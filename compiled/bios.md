## Standard bio (~250 words)

Luka Piškorec is a computational architect and the co-founder of {protocell:labs}, a digital laboratory merging artistic and research practice. He graduated architecture at ETH Zürich in 2011 where he later joined Gramazio Kohler Research to work on the development of robotic digital fabrication techniques and their applications in architecture. The installation Iridescence Print, developed with the group, was exhibited at Palais de Tokyo in Paris in 2015. The same year he co-founded TEN Studio, a Zürich/Belgrade based research and design practice, with which he won the 2018 Swiss Art Awards in Architecture for the project Studiolo. TEN Studio was also shortlisted for the Royal Academy Dorfman Prize in 2024. He held a position as University Teacher (2017–2023) and Lecturer (2024) in Design of Structures at Aalto University, where he taught courses in algorithmic design and digital storytelling. In 2019 he started a PhD at Aalto in generative machine learning for architecture, paused in 2022. His interests as a generative artist involve computational structures, mathematical forms of organization, design algorithms, artificial biology, emergent morphogenesis, generative AI and digital graphics. With {protocell:labs} he has released long-form generative collections on fxhash and Verse, as well as short-form series on Hic et Nunc, Versum, Glitch Forge (now defunct) and Nifty Gateway. He is active in the international generative art community as an artist, developer, commentator, educator and mentor.

## Extended bio (~500 words)

Luka Piškorec is a computational architect, generative artist, researcher and educator working across computational design, robotic fabrication, and algorithmic art. He studied architecture at the University of Zagreb in Croatia and worked in architectural offices in Croatia and Switzerland before continuing his studies at ETH Zürich, where he received his Master of Science in Architecture in 2011. During his studies he concentrated on digital fabrication techniques and algorithmic design applied to architecture.

In 2011 he joined Gramazio Kohler Research at ETH Zürich as a research assistant. From 2011 to 2014 he led the Chair's elective course and elective thesis workshops dealing with the development of robotic digital fabrication techniques and their implementation in architectural design — including projects such as Procedural Wall, Spatial Aggregations, Complex Timber Structures, Depth Modulations, Remote Material Deposition and Iridescence Print, the last of which was exhibited at Palais de Tokyo in Paris in 2015. From 2015 to 2017 he co-organized and taught at the newly formed Master of Advanced Studies (MAS) in Architecture and Digital Fabrication at ETH Zürich.

In 2015 he co-founded TEN Studio, a Zürich/Belgrade based international research and design practice working at the intersection between research and design. With TEN Studio he won the 2018 Swiss Art Awards in Architecture for the project Studiolo, exhibited at Messe Basel. TEN Studio was shortlisted for the Royal Academy Dorfman Prize in 2024.

From 2017 to 2024 he held a position as University Teacher (2017–2023) and Lecturer (2024) in Design of Structures at Aalto University in Helsinki, conducting courses on algorithmic design, digital storytelling, and teaching in architecture studios. In 2019 he started a PhD at Aalto in the field of generative machine learning for architecture, paused in 2022. He has been writing a book on mathematical principles of computational design (unpublished draft).

In 2021 he co-founded {protocell:labs} with Kane Borg, establishing it as a digital laboratory merging artistic and research practice. Within the lab they investigate computational structures, mathematical forms of organization, design algorithms, artificial biology, emergent morphogenesis, generative AI and digital graphics. Their preferred mode of expression is chain-agnostic, long-form generative collections, which they began releasing on the fxhash platform in 2021 — including Monocell (2021), OBSCVRVM, Crystalyx and Chromoplasm (2022), rtrdgtzr and Structura (2023), and TECTONICA, fxFacetune and I Am Not Good At Computer (2024). Collections often feature complex 3D geometry, choreographed animation and interactivity. As a matter of principle, the lab keeps its code open — unminified, unobfuscated, and available on public repositories — and documents its practice through writing and recorded talks.

He is active in the international generative art community as an artist, developer, commentator, educator and mentor, and served on the final jury of Combine24, the generative art competition organized by The Finnish National Gallery in 2024.
